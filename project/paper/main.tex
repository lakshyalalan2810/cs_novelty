\title{Disturbance-Aware Witness Gating for Reliability-Aware LSTM-MPC of a Nonlinear DC Motor}
\author{\IEEEauthorblockN{Anonymous Authors}}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
Speed-sensor faults can corrupt both feedback and learned prediction in
closed-loop LSTM model-predictive control of a nonlinear permanent-magnet
DC motor. We study a reliability-aware architecture with a main
speed-dependent LSTM, a current-informed auxiliary virtual sensor, and a
classical augmented-state extended Kalman filter (EKF) comparator. Frozen V3
evaluation identified a specific weakness: a pure load disturbance could
trigger false entry into sensor-fault handling. V4 therefore gates entry
with a witness that uses voltage and armature current, instantiated
separately by the auxiliary LSTM and EKF. A preregistered nine-hypothesis
confirmatory study executed the exact 12{,}200-cell H1--H9 dependency core
with hierarchical bootstrap inference and Holm correction. Under the tested
0.15~N$\cdot$m load disturbance, both V4 witness variants reduced
false-entry probability relative to V3 C3 by 0.2177; H8 and H9 were the only
multiplicity-supported results. H1--H2 were directionally favorable but not
supported, H3--H4 and H6--H7 had unfavorable observed contrasts, and H5 was
null. Thus witness gating improved entry discrimination for the tested load
confound, while broader improvements in detection, recovery, and tracking
were not established.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Speed-sensor faults corrupt the feedback path of closed-loop motor drives.
Model predictive control (MPC)~\cite{Mayne2000} with learned LSTM
dynamics~\cite{Hochreiter1997} is particularly exposed: a
biased or frozen speed measurement propagates through both the state
estimate and the receding-horizon optimization. Learned residuals offer a
detection signal, but a residual is only useful if its detector separates
true faults from transients.

We evaluated a frozen V3 detector combining an instant residual gate with a
CUSUM test~\cite{Page1954} over 275 closed-loop runs (5 controllers
$\times$ 11 scenarios $\times$ 5 seeds, 29026--29030). On abrupt faults
the protected controller (C3) reduced fault-window RMSE relative to the
unprotected baseline (B) by 95.4\% (bias 15), 87.2\% (bias 5), 98.2\%
(dropout), and 42.6\% (noise); drift showed zero benefit (5.305 vs.\ 5.305
rad/s), combined fault-plus-load improved 8.75\%, parameter variation was
neutral (0.670 vs.\ 0.669~rad/s), and nominal, step, and changing
references showed no degradation. Load disturbance alone degraded tracking
171\% under C3 relative to B (2.539 vs.\ 0.937~rad/s). No optimizer
failure, voltage, slew, or nonfinite event occurred in any run. A post-hoc
decomposition (Table~\ref{tab:posthoc}) showed the apparent detection gap
was dominated by startup false latches (clustered below 1.2~s;
per-training-seed false-latch rates 0.20, 0.68, and 0.00) rather than
missed faults: conditional on a clean start, C3 detected bias and combined
fault-plus-load faults at 100\% across all three training seeds, while
drift detection stayed at 0.091--0.182.

\begin{enumerate}
\item A simulation-based reliability-aware LSTM-MPC framework for nonlinear
PMDC motor speed control under speed-sensor faults.
\item Dual virtual-sensor arbitration combining a speed-dependent main LSTM
with a current-informed auxiliary LSTM.
\item Identification of load-disturbance/sensor-fault confounding as a
failure mode of the frozen V3 reliability detector.
\item Disturbance-aware V4 entry gating instantiated separately with an
auxiliary-LSTM witness and an augmented-state EKF witness.
\item A preregistered nine-hypothesis evaluation showing reduced false
entries for both witnesses at the tested 0.15~N$\cdot$m load condition.
\item Retention of negative and null evidence, including the inactive C2
recovery gate, C4 NO\_GO attribution branch, EKF tradeoffs, training-seed
sensitivity, and the healthy-current-channel limitation.
\end{enumerate}

The preregistered confirmatory study supports reduced load-induced false
entries for both V4 witnesses at the tested 0.15~N$\cdot$m condition, but
does not support the other seven hypotheses after multiplicity correction.
This paper does not claim general superiority; negative results (including
small-bias detection and tracking contrasts) are retained verbatim.

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}
Constrained MPC provides an optimization framework for multivariable control,
but its formal guarantees depend on model and terminal-design assumptions
that are not claimed here~\cite{Mayne2000}. LSTMs provide a learned
stateful sequence model~\cite{Hochreiter1997}. The present work focuses on
how corrupted feedback interacts with the learned model and its reliability
logic.

CUSUM monitoring supplies a sequential residual test~\cite{Page1954}, while
observer- and plausibility-based monitors provide nonlearned comparison
paths. Here these mechanisms are evaluated as bounded engineering
comparators, not as formal fault isolation. The central question is narrower:
whether a current-informed witness can prevent a plant load transient from
being mistaken for a speed-sensor fault without introducing unsupported
claims about the physical fault source.

\section{Method}
\label{sec:method}

\subsection{Plant and control law}
\label{sec:plant}

The plant is a permanent-magnet DC motor with states armature current $i$
and angular speed $\omega$:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:current}
L\frac{di}{dt} = V - Ri - K_b\omega,
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:speed}
J\frac{d\omega}{dt} = K_t i - B\omega - T_f(\omega) - T_L,
\end{equation}
with $R=2~\Omega$, $L=0.5$~H, $K_b=K_t=0.1$,
$J=0.01$~kg$\cdot$m$^2$, $B=0.002$, Coulomb torque $0.02$~N$\cdot$m
smoothed as $0.02\tanh(\omega/0.1)$, and load torque $T_L$. Integration uses RK4 at a
10~ms plant timestep; the controller issues a new voltage every 50~ms (5
plant steps).

The controller is an SLSQP-based MPC~\cite{Mayne2000} with horizon $H=20$,
move blocks $(5,15)$ giving $N_c=2$ free moves, voltage limits 0--12~V, a
maximum step of 2~V per interval, tracking weight 1.0, move weight 0.5,
\texttt{maxiter}=8, tolerance 0.05, and warm start enabled. Adaptation
regions rescale the horizon and step limit: LOW
($H=20$, 2~V), MEDIUM ($H=20$, 1.5~V), HIGH ($H=15$, 1~V). On the reference
machine, measured solve times averaged 68~ms (median 60.6~ms, p95
135.8~ms, max 666.9~ms); 41.6\% of solves met the 50~ms target.

\subsection{Learned predictors}
\label{sec:predictors}

The main predictor is a 2-layer residual LSTM~\cite{Hochreiter1997} with
hidden size 64, dropout 0.2, and 50{,}753 parameters. Its input is a
20-step window of $[\text{voltage}, \text{measured speed}]$. On held-out
test data it achieved RMSE 0.1409~rad/s, MAE 0.1131~rad/s, and $R^2 =
0.9999$. Recursive open-loop rollout error grew with horizon: RMSE
0.211~rad/s at $H=5$, 0.336~rad/s at $H=10$, and 0.488~rad/s at $H=15$.
The network supplies recursive multi-step speed predictions to the MPC.
The frozen residual shortcut adds the final normalized speed input to the
normalized network output before target denormalization. This historical
implementation characteristic was retained rather than altered after
evaluation.

The auxiliary predictor is a 1-layer LSTM with hidden size 32 and
4{,}641 parameters. Its input is a 20-step window of $[\text{voltage},
\text{armature current}]$ with no speed-sensor input, so its estimates
remain independent of speed-sensor corruption by construction. On clean
test data it achieved RMSE 3.44~rad/s. Armature current provides an
additional physical channel correlated with electromagnetic torque and
load-induced motor dynamics; it does not directly measure load.

\subsection{Frozen V3 detector}
\label{sec:v3detector}

The frozen V3 monitor tests the main residual $e_m = y_{\text{meas}} -
\hat{\omega}_{\text{main}}$ with two parallel rules. The instant rule flags
$|e_m|$ above a gate of 0.9372~rad/s, the 99.9th percentile of
clean-validation residuals. The CUSUM rule~\cite{Page1954} accumulates
two-sided deviations about center $-0.0094$ with allowance 0.1404 against
threshold 1.5697. Entry requires 3 consecutive flagged samples; exit
requires 5 consecutive clean samples. Recovery requires both $|e_m|$ within
the gate and the CUSUM score within threshold, and the CUSUM accumulators
reset only after confirmed full recovery. Post-hoc analysis traced the
dominant failure mode to startup false latching
(Fig.~\ref{fig:timeline}).

The feedback selector follows the hierarchy PHYSICAL $\to$ MAIN\_VIRTUAL
$\to$ AUX\_VIRTUAL $\to$ FALLBACK: measured speed while healthy, the main
LSTM estimate while suspect, the auxiliary estimate if the main channel is
unavailable, and a fallback law otherwise.

The historical C2 auxiliary recovery gate produced behavior identical to C1:
its auxiliary condition was never the active recovery constraint. We retain
C2 as an inactive ablation rather than evidence of improvement.

\subsection{V4 detector variants}
\label{sec:v4}

The V4 monitor (Fig.~\ref{fig:architecture}) implements four fixes to the
frozen design: (i) a CUSUM anti-windup clamp with $k=2.0$ bounding score
accumulation; (ii) startup warmup gating that suppresses entries during the
first 150 samples; (iii) a witness AND-rule requiring both main-residual
evidence and witness-agreement evidence before entry; and (iv) bounded
recovery that confines the post-fault discharge transient to a configurable
window. The ablation studies five configurations: full, clamp-only,
gating-only, clamp-plus-gating, and witness-only. The two primary
confirmatory controllers are V4\_full\_aux (auxiliary-LSTM witness) and
V4\_full\_ekf (EKF witness). Their preregistered confirmatory evaluation is
reported in Section~\ref{sec:results}; only the load false-entry hypotheses
survived Holm correction.

For each model pair, witness thresholds were frozen from the 99.9th
percentile of the absolute clean-validation residual
$|y_{\mathrm{meas}}-\hat{\omega}_{\mathrm{witness}}|$. The witness state is
therefore an agreement gate, not a causal label of sensor versus plant
failure. Calibration excluded confirmatory trajectories and H1--H9 outcomes.

A three-way consistency attributor (C4) was implemented and calibrated
during development; it returned output numerically identical to C3 across
all 105 paired development runs, produced zero
LIKELY\_PLANT\_OR\_MAIN\_MISMATCH states, and was gated NO\_GO with seeds
39026--39030 untouched.

\subsection{Classical baselines}
\label{sec:baselines}

E2 is an EKF over the augmented state $[i,\omega,T_L]$ with $T_L$ modeled
as a random walk. It consumes voltage and armature current only, runs a
CUSUM test~\cite{Page1954} on the EKF innovation, and substitutes the EKF
speed estimate while suspect. The filter uses fixed nominal motor
parameters. S1 applies a fixed plausibility gate to $|y_{\text{meas}} -
\hat{\omega}_{\text{EKF}}|$. S2 is a model-free median/rate plausibility
check substituting the median speed. S3 combines a back-EMF Luenberger
observer with a CUSUM test and observer-estimate substitution. Each baseline carries its own independent detector
and substitution source. None of E2/S1/S2/S3 reads measured speed during
substitution; all use the same physical current and voltage inputs as the
auxiliary LSTM.

For nonzero $K_b$, the nominal augmented EKF model is analytically
observable, but its practical observability is weaker over short horizons
and near startup. It is used as a classical baseline and witness, not as a
claimed overall winner.

During development, E2 recorded fault-window RMSE 0.283~rad/s on bias-5 and
dropout faults, versus 0.371~rad/s for C3 and 2.908~rad/s for B on bias-5,
but degraded to 2.971~rad/s under R+30\% parameter variation because the
filter retains fixed nominal parameters (the parameter-transfer estimator
reached 10.69~rad/s); E2 was assigned role gate BASELINE\_ONLY after it
failed paired improvement on combined fault-plus-load in 0/5 seeds.

\section{Experimental Protocol}
\label{sec:protocol}

\subsection{Dataset and training}
\label{sec:dataset}

Training data comprise 120 startup-rich trajectories (startup-from-rest,
closed-loop MPC, closed-loop PI, open-loop multisine, open random-step)
generated under seed 62026 and split 72/24/24 into training, validation,
and test sets. Training uses eleven training seeds (2026--2036).
Normalization statistics come from training windows only. The main and
auxiliary networks each train for at most 200 epochs.

\subsection{Calibration}
\label{sec:calibration}

CUSUM thresholds use a frozen p99.9 grid search with auxiliary/witness
gates; uncertainty comes from a 1000-trajectory bootstrap. Calibration runs
under seed $20260920+\text{training\_seed}$; baseline calibration uses seed
20260921. No test trajectory, fault scenario, or closed-loop result entered
calibration.

\subsection{Confirmatory hypotheses}
\label{sec:hypotheses}

Nine preregistered hypotheses (family $\alpha=0.05$, Holm step-down)
govern the confirmatory study; the full protocol is in
\texttt{V4\_PREREGISTRATION.md}. The original umbrella contained 268{,}910
cells. We executed the exact deduplicated 12{,}200-cell dependency core
needed by H1--H9; the remaining 257{,}260 cells are deferred and contribute
to no confirmatory result. The core contains 9{,}600 fault-free, 2{,}000
sweep, and 600 recovery cells.
\begin{enumerate}
\item[H1.] V4\_full\_aux false-latch probability is lower than C3 on
fault-free pairs.
\item[H2.] V4\_full\_ekf false-latch probability is lower than C3 on
fault-free pairs.
\item[H3.] V4\_full\_aux detection probability exceeds C3 at bias $2\sigma$
on clean-start pairs.
\item[H4.] V4\_full\_ekf detection probability exceeds C3 at bias $2\sigma$
on clean-start pairs.
\item[H5.] V4\_full\_aux recovery probability exceeds C3 at bias $8\sigma$
on clean-start pairs.
\item[H6.] V4\_full\_aux fault-window RMSE is lower than B at bias $8\sigma$
(intention-to-treat).
\item[H7.] The V4\_full\_aux tracking penalty versus B is smaller than the
C3 penalty versus B (intention-to-treat).
\item[H8.] V4\_full\_aux load false-entry probability is lower than C3 at
0.15~N$\cdot$m.
\item[H9.] V4\_full\_ekf load false-entry probability is lower than C3 at
0.15~N$\cdot$m.
\end{enumerate}
Paired comparisons share $(\text{training\_seed}, \text{sim\_seed})$ pairs.
For conditional endpoints (H3--H5, H8--H9), pairs in which either controller
pre-latched are excluded (clean-start pair-exclusion rule); H6--H7 use
intention-to-treat with no exclusion.

Inference uses 20{,}000 hierarchical-bootstrap replicates: training seeds are
resampled first, then simulation-seed clusters within each selected training
seed, with all reference rows retained in a selected cluster. The 95\% CI is
the uncentered percentile interval of bootstrap means. The two-sided empirical
$p$-value instead uses absolute tails after centering the replicates at their
bootstrap mean under the zero paired-mean null. These different constructions
need not invert exactly, particularly with three top-level clusters and
discrete outcomes. Holm step-down~\cite{Holm1979} controls the
nine-hypothesis family at 0.05. Cohen's paired $d_z$ is the mean retained
delta divided by its sample standard deviation; it is undefined when all
deltas are zero. Zero empirical tail hits are displayed as a finite
Monte Carlo bound rather than as $p=0$.

\subsection{Evaluation seeds}
\label{sec:seeds}

Confirmatory core seeds occupy fresh blocks: 71000--71199 for fault-free
runs, 72000--72049 for the required sweep cells, and 72000--72099 for the
recovery cells. These blocks are distinct from all development blocks used in
prior V3/C4 studies. Confirmatory analysis runs in
\texttt{v4\_confirm\_analysis.py} with Holm step-down correction.

An initial 400-cell attempt was discarded before any confirmatory analysis
after a plan-file provenance defect was detected. The scientific plan was
independently reconstructed 12{,}200/12{,}200 and execution restarted from
zero under an administratively re-frozen immutable plan. Pre-analysis
integrity checking then found that the EKF witness had not been initialized
in all 2{,}700 required V4\_full\_ekf cells. We fixed that lifecycle defect
and reran all and only those corrupted cells before the single confirmatory
analysis; the controller-identity/failure-signature predicate was independent
of performance outcomes, and the other 9{,}500 cells were not rerun. The final core contains no
missing, duplicate, unexpected, optimizer-failure, observer-failure,
nonfinite, or voltage-failure record.

A final audit found that the historical implementation resampled individual
reference rows rather than simulation-seed clusters for H1, H2, and H7.
We preserved those outputs and recomputed only the affected summaries from the
unchanged canonical core using the hierarchy above. No simulation, endpoint,
hypothesis, multiplicity rule, or decision changed.

\subsection{Historical branch and robustness context}

The historical branches provide boundary evidence rather than additional V4
hypotheses. C2 was behaviorally identical to C1 because its added recovery
condition never bound. C4 failed its preregistered development gates and
remains NO\_GO: its three pairwise scalar distances contain only two
independent degrees of freedom, and the intended attribution geometry did not
appear. The augmented-state EKF remains BASELINE\_ONLY: it was strong on some
isolated sensor faults but weaker under load and parameter mismatch.

Across historical C3 training seeds 2026--2028, the mean combined-fault
C3-minus-B RMSE contrast was 0.311, $-1.555$, and $-0.461$~rad/s,
respectively. Because one learned-model realization was unfavorable, the
frozen verdict is TRAINING-SEED-SENSITIVE. In the final V3 severity study, no
tested severity earned strict universal support, and corruption of the
assumed-healthy current channel degraded both C3 and EKF.

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

The supported result addresses the failure that motivated V4. The V3
residual detector sometimes conflated a plant load transient with
speed-sensor corruption and entered substitution unnecessarily. At the
preregistered 0.15~N$\cdot$m load condition, an agreement requirement based
on either the auxiliary learned witness or the classical EKF witness removed
those false entries in the analyzed clean-start pairs. Agreement across two
different witness implementations strengthens the narrow interpretation that
current-informed consistency can protect entry logic against this tested
confound.

That agreement is not proof of causal fault isolation. Both witnesses use the
same physical current channel and both can fail under current corruption,
parameter mismatch, startup transients, or untested disturbances. The
unfavorable H3/H4/H6/H7 contrasts and null H5 result further show that better
entry discrimination did not translate into general improvements in
detection, recovery, or tracking. These negative results define where the
mechanism helped and where additional evidence is still required.

\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}
The confirmatory evidence is limited to the executed fault-free,
bias-$2\sigma$, bias-$8\sigma$, and load-0.15 conditions. Drift detection
remains poor in frozen historical evidence; dropout, drift, other severities,
combined-fault V4 expansions, robustness OFAT, timing, and non-bias recovery
were deferred. Direct historical C3 robustness comparisons include only three
training seeds and show training-seed sensitivity. Parameter mismatch remains
an established weakness for both current-informed learned estimation and the
fixed-parameter EKF.

Both witnesses assume a healthy current channel, so current-sensor faults
remain outside supported tolerance claims. The core retained 3{,}053
slew-limit counters as observed safety outcomes rather than exclusions. MPC
solve times exceed the control period on the reference machine, preventing a
20~Hz real-time claim. All results are simulation-only on one motor model,
with no HIL or hardware validation, formal stability proof, or formal
fault-isolation proof. Bootstrap intervals and $p$-values have finite
Monte Carlo resolution and only three top-level training-seed clusters for
H1--H5 and H7--H9. Finally, the administrative re-freeze, 2{,}700-cell
outcome-independent EKF repair, and H1/H2/H7 bootstrap correction are
necessary provenance disclosures; they did not alter the core scientific
design or the conclusion that only H8/H9 reject.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}
Reliability-aware learned MPC benefited from explicit disturbance-aware
witness gating, but the value was mechanism-specific rather than universal.
Under preregistered confirmatory evaluation, both V4 witness designs reduced
load-induced false sensor-fault entries at the tested disturbance magnitude,
while broader improvements in detection, recovery, and tracking were not
established. Future work should test broader fault severities,
current-channel faults, HIL and hardware execution, and formal robustness and
stability properties.
