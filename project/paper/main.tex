\begin{abstract}
Speed-sensor faults degrade closed-loop LSTM model-predictive control
of a permanent-magnet DC motor by corrupting the feedback signal.
This paper evaluates a reliability-aware architecture in which learned
residuals gate feedback substitution, cross-checked by two independent
witnesses, an auxiliary current-fed LSTM and an extended Kalman filter,
neither of which consumes measured speed. The frozen V3 detector reduced
fault-window RMSE by 87.2--98.2\% on abrupt bias and dropout faults
relative to the unprotected baseline, conditional on a clean start, but
provided zero benefit on drift and degraded load-disturbance tracking by
171\%. A post-hoc decomposition showed the frozen detector's apparent
detection gap was dominated by startup false latching rather than missed
faults: conditional on a clean start, bias detection reached 100\% across
three training seeds. V4 introduces a CUSUM anti-windup clamp, startup
warmup gating, a witness-agreement AND rule, and bounded recovery
discharge. Efficacy is tested in a preregistered confirmatory study with
nine Holm-corrected hypotheses on fresh seeds. [PENDING: V4 confirmatory]
Confirmatory results will determine whether V4 reduces false latching and
preserves detection. [PENDING: V4 confirmatory]
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Speed-sensor faults corrupt the feedback path of closed-loop motor drives.
Model predictive control (MPC)~\cite{Mayne2000} with learned LSTM
dynamics~\cite{Hochreiter1997,Zarzycki2022} is particularly exposed: a
biased or frozen speed measurement propagates through both the state
estimate and the receding-horizon optimization. Learned residuals offer a
detection signal, but a residual is only useful if its detector separates
true faults from transients.

We evaluated a frozen V3 detector combining an instant residual gate with a
CUSUM test~\cite{Page1954} over 275 closed-loop runs (5 controllers
$\times$ 11 scenarios $\times$ 5 seeds, 29026--29030). On abrupt faults
the protected controller (C3) reduced fault-window RMSE relative to the
unprotected baseline (B) by 95.4\% (bias 15), 87.2\% (bias 5), 98.2\%
(dropout), and 42.6\% (noise); drift showed zero benefit (5.305 vs.\ 5.305
rad/s), combined fault-plus-load improved 8.75\%, parameter variation was
neutral (0.670 vs.\ 0.669~rad/s), and nominal, step, and changing
references showed no degradation. Load disturbance alone degraded tracking
171\% under C3 relative to B (2.539 vs.\ 0.937~rad/s). No optimizer
failure, voltage, slew, or nonfinite event occurred in any run. A post-hoc
decomposition (Table~\ref{tab:posthoc}) showed the apparent detection gap
was dominated by startup false latches (clustered below 1.2~s;
per-training-seed false-latch rates 0.20, 0.68, and 0.00) rather than
missed faults: conditional on a clean start, C3 detected bias and combined
fault-plus-load faults at 100\% across all three training seeds, while
drift detection stayed at 0.091--0.182.

\begin{enumerate}
\item Post-hoc decomposition separating false latching from missed
detection in the frozen V3 detector.
\item V4 detector variants (CUSUM anti-windup clamp k=2.0, 150-sample
warmup gating, witness AND-rule, bounded recovery discharge) with a
factorial ablation.
\item Classical baselines E2 (EKF+CUSUM), S1 (fixed gate), S2
(median/rate plausibility), S3 (Luenberger+CUSUM), each with its own
independent detector and substitution source---none reading measured speed
during substitution.
\item Preregistered confirmatory protocol with 9 hypotheses, Holm
correction, and clean-start pair-exclusion rule.
\end{enumerate}

Whether the V4 variants reduce false latching and preserve detection on
fresh seeds is tested by the preregistered confirmatory study. [PENDING: V4
confirmatory] This paper does not claim general superiority; negative
results (drift, load disturbance, training-seed sensitivity) are retained
verbatim.

\section{Method}
\label{sec:method}

\subsection{Plant and control law}
\label{sec:plant}

The plant is a permanent-magnet DC motor with states armature current $i$
and angular speed $\omega$:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:current}
L\frac{di}{dt} = V - Ri - K_b\omega,
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:speed}
J\frac{d\omega}{dt} = K_t i - B\omega - T_f(\omega) - T_L,
\end{equation}
with $R=2~\Omega$, $L=0.5$~H, $K_b=K_t=0.1$,
$J=0.01$~kg$\cdot$m$^2$, $B=0.002$, Coulomb torque $0.02$~N$\cdot$m
smoothed via $\tanh$, and load torque $T_L$. Integration uses RK4 at a
10~ms plant timestep; the controller issues a new voltage every 50~ms (5
plant steps).

The controller is an SLSQP-based MPC~\cite{Mayne2000} with horizon $H=20$,
move blocks $(5,15)$ giving $N_c=2$ free moves, voltage limits 0--12~V, a
maximum step of 2~V per interval, \texttt{maxiter}=8, and warm start
enabled. Adaptation regions rescale the horizon and step limit: LOW
($H=20$, 2~V), MEDIUM ($H=20$, 1.5~V), HIGH ($H=15$, 1~V). On the reference
machine, measured solve times averaged 68~ms (median 60.6~ms, p95
135.8~ms, max 666.9~ms); 41.6\% of solves met the 50~ms target.

\subsection{Learned predictors}
\label{sec:predictors}

The main predictor is a 2-layer residual LSTM~\cite{Hochreiter1997} with
hidden size 64, dropout 0.2, and 50{,}753 parameters. Its input is a
20-step window of $[\text{voltage}, \text{measured speed}]$. On held-out
test data it achieved RMSE 0.1409~rad/s, MAE 0.1131~rad/s, and $R^2 =
0.9999$. Recursive open-loop rollout error grew with horizon: RMSE
0.211~rad/s at $H=5$, 0.336~rad/s at $H=10$, and 0.488~rad/s at $H=15$.
The network supplies multi-step speed predictions to the MPC, an LSTM-MPC
arrangement consistent with trajectory-linearization
practice~\cite{Zarzycki2022}.

The auxiliary predictor is a 1-layer LSTM with hidden size 32 and
4{,}641 parameters. Its input is a 20-step window of $[\text{voltage},
\text{armature current}]$ with no speed-sensor input, so its estimates
remain independent of speed-sensor corruption by construction. On clean
test data it achieved RMSE 3.44~rad/s.

\subsection{Frozen V3 detector}
\label{sec:v3detector}

The frozen V3 monitor tests the main residual $e_m = y_{\text{meas}} -
\hat{\omega}_{\text{main}}$ with two parallel rules. The instant rule flags
$|e_m|$ above a gate of 0.9372~rad/s, the 99.9th percentile of
clean-validation residuals. The CUSUM rule~\cite{Page1954} accumulates
two-sided deviations about center $-0.0094$ with allowance 0.1404 against
threshold 1.5697. Entry requires 3 consecutive flagged samples; exit
requires 5 consecutive clean samples. Recovery requires both $|e_m|$ within
the gate and the CUSUM score within threshold, and the CUSUM accumulators
reset only after confirmed full recovery. Post-hoc analysis traced the
dominant failure mode to startup false latching
(Fig.~\ref{fig:timeline}).

The feedback selector follows the hierarchy PHYSICAL $\to$ MAIN\_VIRTUAL
$\to$ AUX\_VIRTUAL $\to$ FALLBACK: measured speed while healthy, the main
LSTM estimate while suspect, the auxiliary estimate if the main channel is
unavailable, and a fallback law otherwise.

\subsection{V4 detector variants}
\label{sec:v4}

The V4 monitor (Fig.~\ref{fig:architecture}) implements four fixes to the
frozen design: (i) a CUSUM anti-windup clamp with $k=2.0$ bounding score
accumulation; (ii) startup warmup gating that suppresses entries during the
first 150 samples; (iii) a witness AND-rule requiring both main-residual
evidence and witness-agreement evidence before entry; and (iv) bounded
recovery that confines the post-fault discharge transient to a configurable
window. The ablation studies five configurations: full, clamp-only,
gating-only, clamp-plus-gating, and witness-only. The two primary
confirmatory controllers are V4\_full\_aux (auxiliary-LSTM witness) and
V4\_full\_ekf (EKF witness). Whether these variants reduce false latching
and preserve detection relative to C3 is tested by the preregistered
confirmatory study. [PENDING: V4 confirmatory]

A three-way consistency attributor (C4) was implemented and calibrated
during development; it returned output numerically identical to C3 across
all 105 paired development runs, produced zero
LIKELY\_PLANT\_OR\_MAIN\_MISMATCH states, and was gated NO\_GO with seeds
39026--39030 untouched.

\subsection{Classical baselines}
\label{sec:baselines}

E2 is an EKF over the augmented state $[i,\omega,T_L]$ with $T_L$ modeled
as a random walk. It consumes voltage and armature current only, runs a
CUSUM test~\cite{Page1954} on the EKF innovation, and substitutes the EKF
speed estimate while suspect. The filter uses fixed nominal motor
parameters. S1 applies a fixed plausibility gate to $|y_{\text{meas}} -
\hat{\omega}_{\text{EKF}}|$. S2 is a model-free median/rate plausibility
check substituting the median speed. S3 combines a back-EMF Luenberger
observer with a CUSUM test and observer-estimate substitution, following
standard observer-based fault-diagnosis practice (TODO:cite---Luenberger/EKF
fault-detection survey). Each baseline carries its own independent detector
and substitution source. None of E2/S1/S2/S3 reads measured speed during
substitution; all use the same physical current and voltage inputs as the
auxiliary LSTM.

During development, E2 recorded fault-window RMSE 0.283~rad/s on bias-5 and
dropout faults, versus 0.371~rad/s for C3 and 2.908~rad/s for B on bias-5,
but degraded to 2.971~rad/s under R+30\% parameter variation because the
filter retains fixed nominal parameters (the parameter-transfer estimator
reached 10.69~rad/s); E2 was assigned role gate BASELINE\_ONLY after it
failed paired improvement on combined fault-plus-load in 0/5 seeds.

\section{Experimental Protocol}
\label{sec:protocol}

\subsection{Dataset and training}
\label{sec:dataset}

Training data comprise 120 startup-rich trajectories (startup-from-rest,
closed-loop MPC, closed-loop PI, open-loop multisine, open random-step)
generated under seed 62026 and split 72/24/24 into training, validation,
and test sets. Training uses eleven training seeds (2026--2036).
Normalization statistics come from training windows only. The main and
auxiliary networks each train for at most 200 epochs.

\subsection{Calibration}
\label{sec:calibration}

CUSUM thresholds use a frozen p99.9 grid search with auxiliary/witness
gates; uncertainty comes from a 1000-trajectory bootstrap. Calibration runs
under seed $20260920+\text{training\_seed}$; baseline calibration uses seed
20260921. No test trajectory, fault scenario, or closed-loop result entered
calibration.

\subsection{Confirmatory hypotheses}
\label{sec:hypotheses}

Nine preregistered hypotheses (family $\alpha=0.05$, Holm step-down)
govern the confirmatory study; the full protocol is in
\texttt{V4\_PREREGISTRATION.md}. The study planned approximately
268{,}910 confirmatory runs and was preregistered before any confirmatory
seed was executed.
\begin{enumerate}
\item[H1.] V4\_full\_aux false-latch probability is lower than C3 on
fault-free pairs.
\item[H2.] V4\_full\_ekf false-latch probability is lower than C3 on
fault-free pairs.
\item[H3.] V4\_full\_aux detection probability exceeds C3 at bias $2\sigma$
on clean-start pairs.
\item[H4.] V4\_full\_ekf detection probability exceeds C3 at bias $2\sigma$
on clean-start pairs.
\item[H5.] V4\_full\_aux recovery probability exceeds C3 at bias $8\sigma$
on clean-start pairs.
\item[H6.] V4\_full\_aux fault-window RMSE is lower than B at bias $8\sigma$
(intention-to-treat).
\item[H7.] The V4\_full\_aux tracking penalty versus B is smaller than the
C3 penalty versus B (intention-to-treat).
\item[H8.] V4\_full\_aux load false-entry probability is lower than C3 at
0.15~N$\cdot$m.
\item[H9.] V4\_full\_ekf load false-entry probability is lower than C3 at
0.15~N$\cdot$m.
\end{enumerate}
Paired comparisons share $(\text{training\_seed}, \text{sim\_seed})$ pairs.
For conditional endpoints (H3--H5, H8--H9), pairs in which either controller
pre-latched are excluded (clean-start pair-exclusion rule); H6--H7 use
intention-to-treat with no exclusion.

\subsection{Evaluation seeds}
\label{sec:seeds}

Confirmatory seeds occupy fresh blocks: 71000--71999 for fault-free runs
(200 seeds), 72000--72049 for the magnitude sweep, and a 100-seed recovery
block. These blocks are distinct from all development blocks used in prior
V3/C4 studies. Confirmatory analysis runs in
\texttt{v4\_confirm\_analysis.py} with Holm step-down correction.
